# (b) One-page talking script for the owner part
Read in plain words. Numbers come from the master register and proposal-vs-reality.md. Do not add rupee figures for the fixes (see section 0, point 2).
Opening (about 1 minute). "Thank you for the time. In half an hour I will tell you whether RadCore is safe to run your money, GST and salaries on, what is wrong, what it will take to fix, and then I need ten decisions from you. None of what I say needs technical knowledge. If something is unclear, stop me."
The verdict (about 3 minutes). "Short answer: not yet. The design underneath is good, better than most software of its kind. But if you put your real accounts, GST returns and salaries on it today, you would be exposed in six ways, and the first one could cost you everything. We read the whole code. The first look in the proposal listed 183 items. The full review found 387 findings, and after combining the ones that share a cause there are 222 fixes. One is critical. Fifty-nine are serious. Nothing here shows on the screens. These problems appear on a bad day: a server failure, an unusual sequence of clicks, someone with more access than they should have."
What is safe (about 3 minutes). "These are sound. Every sale and payment is written on both sides of the books and the database refuses an entry that does not balance. Issued invoices cannot be edited and invoice numbers have no gaps. Two people cannot both sell the last unit. Money is held exactly, not rounded by the computer. And there are about 520 automatic tests, more than the 430 the proposal said. Also, the team already fixed several things on 26 October that the proposal still lists as open: leave above the allowance is now unpaid, payslip links hide the bank account and expire, the scheduler runs every 15 minutes, a second person is needed to reopen a closed year. We will correct the proposal on those points."
What is not safe (about 5 minutes). "Six things.One, backups. As shipped, the only copy is on the same disk as the database, not encrypted, and the test that is meant to prove a restore works would pass even on an empty database. One disk failure or a ransomware attack and the books, GST records and payroll go. This is the one critical item.Two, GST. Credit can be claimed on bills from suppliers who are not registered, which makes your return wrong. There is no reverse charge. The filing file has never been tested on the portal. The lock on filed months covers only part of the ways in.Three, salaries. The tax on salary is a number someone types. PF and ESIC are worked from an assumption that basic pay is half of gross. Controls on who can approve and pay are better than before but still weak.Four, people's private data. There is no way to delete a person's data on request, and the logs keep copies. Your law adviser needs to look at this.Five, e-invoicing and e-way bills are not built. Whether you must have them depends on your turnover. Your accountant must tell us.Six, where it runs. The only live copy is in Singapore, and you promised India."
The three waves (about 4 minutes). "We split the fixes into three. Wave 1, before any real data goes in: 49 fixes, about 120 working days of one developer. Wave 2, before you switch off Vyapar: 119 fixes, about 265 days. Wave 3, the first three months after: 54 fixes, about 163 days. All together about 549 working days. For scale, 120 days is about 24 working weeks for one developer on this alone. With more developers it is faster, but not in the same proportion. That is why the pace is your decision."
Money and time (about 3 minutes). "I can give you days, not rupees; the day rate is between you and your developer. Not in those days: your chartered accountant's time, your law adviser's time, and any AI features. Running cost: the host we recommend, in Mumbai, is about 72 US dollars a month today, around Rs 6,860, or about Rs 8,090 with GST, which you can normally claim back; confirm that with your accountant."
Decisions (about 7 minutes). "I will show a table. Ten need an answer today or this week: whether you accept the fixes we already made, where it runs, one company or several, whether e-invoicing applies to you, whether you want AI and with whom, how fast to go, who the second approver is for salaries and year reopening, the budget, and who we ask at the accountant's firm and at the law adviser's. For each there is a recommended default, and what happens if we wait. If you are unsure, take the default and we revisit it at the next call."
Close (about 1 minute). "You do not need to read the technical reports. The developer will. What I need from you after this call: send the cover note to your accountant, and send me your last three years' turnover, so we can settle the e-invoice question first."
If asked "Is it going to be OK?" "Yes, with work. The foundation is the hard part and it is good. The gaps are known, listed and costed."
